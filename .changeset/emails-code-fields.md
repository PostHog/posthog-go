A `CodeEmail` takes `Cc`, `Bcc` and `Attachments`. `Cc` and `Bcc` are lists of bare addresses, at most 10 each. An `EmailAttachment` is a `Filename` and its `Content` bytes, with an optional `ContentType` and `ContentID` (to show the file inline as `cid:<ContentID>`). An email carries at most 10 attachments and at most 40 MB after Base64 encoding, counting the bodies and the encoded subject as PostHog does, about 29 MB of files; `Send` checks both before any request and fails with `too_many_attachments` or `message_too_large`. It then hashes each file with SHA-256, asks PostHog which files it lacks, uploads each of those once, straight to PostHog's file storage without the secret key, and sends the email naming each file by its hash. PostHog keeps uploaded files for its retention period, and a file it already has isn't uploaded again, and an upload URL that expired while earlier files uploaded is asked for again. A failed upload fails with `attachment_upload_failed` and the storage's answer, or with `network_error` when storage can't be reached, naming the attachment in `Field`, and no email is sent; a storage 5xx, 429 or `RequestTimeout` and a network error are retried first, waiting as long as storage's `Retry-After` asks. A refusal of the upload names the attachments in `Field` as `Attachments` or `Attachments[i]`. The day's upload quota running out fails with `upload_quota_exceeded`, named `EmailErrorDailyQuotaExceeded`, and isn't retried. An attachment without a `Filename` or `Content` fails with `invalid_attachment` before any request, and no error holds a file's content or an upload URL. Empty lists are left out of the request. A PostHog version that doesn't take `Cc` or `Bcc` yet refuses the send with an error that names the field, instead of sending without it; one without the upload route, or a project that can't send email from code, answers `not_found` on `Attachments` before any upload, and nothing is sent.
